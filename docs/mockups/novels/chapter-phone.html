The Ferry at Yeji Chapter 4 of 12
Chapter 4

The Radio

Ama Serwaa · about 19 minutes

The stranger did not ask for Akua. He asked for the woman who kept the ferry tickets, and when Maame Esi pointed at the kiosk he walked to it slowly, as if the ground might change its mind about holding him.

He put the radio on the counter. It was the green one, with the dent in the corner where Kwabena had dropped it the year the lake rose, and the tape over the battery door that said, in his square handwriting, do not open, the spirits are inside.

“Where did you get this?” Akua said. Her voice came out smaller than she meant it to.

“From a man in Makango,” the stranger said. “He told me to bring it to the girl at Yeji who counts the fishermen. He said she would know the song.”

⁂

That night she turned it on for the first time in four years. It found the station on its own, the way it always had, and through the hiss a choir was singing the old song about the river:

Asuo yi, asuo yi,
wo na wode me ba kɔeɛ,
fa no bra.

She sat with it until the battery died, and then she sat with the silence, which was worse, because now it sounded like him.
